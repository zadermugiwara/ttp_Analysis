\title{Single Production of a Vector-Like Top Partner at a Leptonic Collider via the Recoil-Mass Technique}

\author[a]{Higinio Valle}
\author[a]{Cristina Oropeza}
\author[a]{Oscar Ochoa}

\affiliation[a]{Universidad Iberoamericana, CDMX, México}

\date{\today}

\abstract{We revisit the single production of a vector-like top partner $T$ at
a $\sqrt{s}=\SI{3}{TeV}$ $e^{+}e^{-}$ collider using a mass-agnostic selection
built around the recoil-mass observable. We validate the baseline selection
without beam polarization or initial-state radiation (ISR), quantify the impact
of realistic running that includes ISR together with an $e^{-}$ beam
polarization of $+80\%$, and map the discovery reach as a function of the top
partner mass $m_{T}$ and coupling $\kappa$. The analysis relies on
fully-simulated samples processed through a common reconstruction and event
selection chain, using the yields extracted from the project's ROOT files.}

\keywords{Vector-like quarks, Electron-positron collider, Recoil mass}

\section{Introduction}
Vector-like quarks remain a motivated extension of the Standard Model,
addressing the sensitivity of the Higgs potential to top-quark loops and
appearing in models such as composite Higgs, little Higgs, and extra
dimensions.  A clean $e^{+}e^{-}$ environment provides complementary reach to
hadron colliders, especially for single production which depends on an
effective coupling $\kappa$ controlling the $T$--top--gauge boson vertex.
Following the strategy proposed for recoil-based searches, we adopt a
recoil-mass based selection capable of targeting a broad mass hypothesis without
fine-tuned signal windows.

\section{Model and parameters}
The analysis targets benchmark masses $m_{T}\in\{1.2,1.6,2.0,2.4\}\,\si{TeV}$
with widths derived from the simulation campaign.
Table~\ref{tab:Tt1200Kappa}--\ref{tab:TtNoKappaGrouped} (see
App.~\ref{app:widths}) summarize the $\kappa$-dependent widths extracted from
the ROOT production chain.  Unless otherwise stated, the coupling is set to
$\kappa=0.20$ for the benchmark sensitivity figures, corresponding to a total
width of \SI{22.3}{GeV} for $m_{T} = \SI{1.2}{TeV}$.

\section{Event generation and reconstruction}
Signal events $e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow T\,\bar{t}$ are generated at parton level
with \textsc{MadGraph5\_aMC@NLO}, decayed with \textsc{MadSpin}, and showered
with \textsc{Pythia 8}.
Standard Model backgrounds include $t\bar{t}$, $t\bar{t}Z$, $t\bar{t}h$,
$W^{+}W^{-}Z$, and leptonic single-top channels, all normalized to an integrated
luminosity of $L=\SI{5}{ab^{-1}}$.  The detector response and reconstruction are
shared across all production modes; the resulting ROOT files store the relevant
histograms and metadata.

\section{Selection strategy}
The recoil mass is computed event-by-event by solving
\begin{equation}
  m_{\text{recoil}}^{2} = s - 2\,E_{\text{top}}\sqrt{s} + m_{\text{top}}^{2},
  \label{eq:recoilMass}
\end{equation}
where $E_{\text{top}}$ and $m_{\text{top}}$ are reconstructed from the
leading large-$R$ jet that passes a top-tag requirement.  The baseline
selection demands
\begin{itemize}
  \item at least one high-$p_{T}$ Cambridge--Aachen $R=1.0$ jet tagged with the
        Johns Hopkins top-tagger to identify boosted hadronic top candidates,
  \item at least one $b$-tagged subjet or small-$R$ jet in the event,
  \item missing energy $E_{\text{miss}} < \SI{1}{TeV}$, and
  \item $m_{\text{recoil}} > \SI{1.1}{TeV}$ to suppress the Standard Model
        continuum.
\end{itemize}

The isolation veto eliminates events containing isolated energetic leptons,
thereby removing semileptonic top decays and diboson channels that would mimic
the boosted-top signature while evading the recoil-mass requirement.

\section{Impact of ISR and polarization}
To emulate a more realistic running scenario we repeat the analysis on the
samples that include ISR together with an
electron-beam polarization of $+80\%$ (positrons unpolarized).  Relative to the
baseline, the background yield increases to $B=\num{9789}$ events due to ISR,
while the signal yield decreases because of the modified production cross
section.  Figure~\ref{fig:comparison-recoil} compares the total signal-plus-
background expectation in the two configurations.  The significance loss is
summarized in Table~\ref{tab:significance-summary}; for $m_{T}=\SI{1.2}{TeV}$
the discovery reach drops from $Z=2.33$ to $Z=1.72$.

\section{Scanning \texorpdfstring{$\kappa$}{kappa}}
The dedicated significance scan aggregates the
significances obtained from all dedicated $\kappa$ samples.  The resulting map
in the $(m_{T},\kappa)$ plane is visualized in Fig.~\ref{fig:kappa-scan-baseline}.  The baseline
configuration reaches $Z\simeq 5$ for $(m_{T},\kappa)=(\SI{1.2}{TeV},0.30)$, and
the sensitivity falls below $Z=1$ once $m_{T}\gtrsim\SI{2}{TeV}$ unless
$\kappa\gtrsim 0.4$.  These contours provide a transparent summary that can be
reinterpreted for specific decay modes by rescaling the signal efficiencies.

\section{Discussion and outlook}
The recoil-based selection retains robustness against variations in the true top
partner mass and already yields $\mathcal{O}(2\sigma)$ sensitivity at
$\kappa=0.20$ without ISR or beam polarization.  Including ISR and realistic
polarization reduces the reach by roughly $25\%$ in significance, motivating the
use of optimized polarization configurations and multivariate discriminants
that incorporate ISR-sensitive observables.  Extending the scan to larger
luminosities or combining with complementary decay channels will be crucial for
excluding or discovering the full parameter space suggested by
Figs.~\ref{fig:kappa-scan-baseline} and \ref{fig:kappa-scan-isr}.
